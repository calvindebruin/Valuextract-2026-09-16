# ValueXtract Agri opportunity catalog

Use this catalog as a structured search space after completing the financial and agricultural analysis. Include an opportunity only when there is a defensible link from evidence to commercial action. Consolidate related triggers into one engagement when they share the same work programme and value mechanism.

## A. Tax advisory

Potential triggers:

- assessed losses, unusual effective tax positions, deferred-tax movements, or unutilised tax balances;
- fragmented farming entities, holding companies, property companies, trusts, related parties, or shareholder funding;
- significant qualifying capex, equipment, renewable-energy assets, or farming expenditure;
- material VAT balances, diesel usage, export activities, asset disposals, succession, or cross-border transactions.

Potential services:

- assessed-loss and group-utilisation review;
- farming entity, holding-company, trust/company, and intercompany tax-structure review;
- capital, wear-and-tear, farming, and capex allowance review;
- renewable-energy incentive, diesel-rebate, and VAT control review;
- deferred-tax, estate, succession, CGT, disposal, export, and cross-border tax planning.

Do not assert an allowance, incentive, rebate, or tax result from a financial statement line alone. State `Potential eligibility should be investigated` and specify the records and current law that must be tested.

## B. Agricultural funding and capital

Potential triggers:

- seasonal cash deficits, long production cycles, slow collections, inventory build-up, or growth capex;
- material debtors, export receivables, crops, livestock, inventory, land, property, equipment, vehicles, packhouses, or offtake agreements that may support financing;
- orchard establishment, irrigation, packhouse, cold-storage, equipment, energy, or expansion plans;
- capital constraints alongside a credible path to service funding.

Potential services:

- seasonal production, working-capital, revolving, crop, debtor, invoice-discounting, offtake-backed, and inventory finance;
- equipment, tractor, vehicle, asset-backed, land-backed, and mortgage finance;
- orchard, irrigation, packhouse, cold-storage, and renewable-energy finance;
- expansion capital, growth equity, private credit, development or DFI funding;
- grant, transformation, B-BBEE, or ESD funding eligibility and application support.

Never state that finance or a grant is available without current supporting evidence. Separate funding capacity from client value: capital raised is not automatically economic value.

## C. Debt restructuring

Potential triggers:

- high or rapidly rising interest expense;
- weak interest coverage, high leverage, or constrained debt-service ability;
- multiple facilities, expensive short-term funding, persistent overdraft usage, maturity concentration, or fragmented asset finance;
- a mismatch between repayment profile and the agricultural cash cycle.

Potential services:

- facility mapping and lender-ready financial model;
- debt consolidation, refinancing, term extension, and interest-rate or covenant negotiation;
- alternative funder and asset-backed finance assessment;
- seasonal facility design and restructuring of capital repayments around crop cash flows.

Quantify value from supported interest, fee, cash-flow, or risk-capacity scenarios rather than assuming an unverified market rate.

## D. Treasury and foreign exchange

Potential triggers:

- export revenue, foreign-currency receipts, imported inputs, FX gains/losses, currency-denominated debt, or cash-flow volatility;
- disclosed USD, EUR, GBP, or other currency exposure.

Potential services:

- FX exposure and sensitivity assessment;
- forward-exchange and natural-hedging evaluation;
- treasury policy, currency matching, export-receipt strategy, and cash-management optimisation.

Do not assume export markets, hedge rates, or derivatives eligibility. Quantify only exposures and scenarios supported by the data.

## E. Insurance and risk management

Potential triggers:

- crop, livestock, equipment, property, transit, marine, export, credit, liability, business-interruption, or key-person exposures evidenced by the operation;
- concentration in a crop, location, customer, route, facility, or essential person;
- material uninsured or underinsured assets suggested by the asset base and insurance schedule.

Potential services:

- crop, hail, drought, weather, livestock, equipment, asset, and business-interruption review;
- goods-in-transit, marine/export, credit, key-person, and liability cover review;
- insurance-value and business-interruption modelling.

Identify only needs supported by business characteristics. Do not conclude that the entity lacks cover unless the insurance schedule confirms it.

## F. Energy

Potential triggers:

- material or rising electricity and diesel costs;
- irrigation pumping, packhouse cooling, cold storage, refrigeration, processing, or generator exposure;
- supply reliability constraints stated in the evidence.

Potential services:

- load-profile and energy-efficiency review;
- solar PV, battery storage, wheeling, or power-purchase-agreement feasibility;
- renewable-energy finance and generator optimisation.

Value the opportunity from a site-specific baseline and scenarios. Do not infer consumption, tariffs, system size, or savings from the expense line alone.

## G. Water and irrigation

Potential triggers:

- irrigated operations, significant pumping or water costs, boreholes, dams, or water infrastructure;
- disclosed water-security, water-right, capacity, or reliability risks.

Potential services:

- irrigation and pumping optimisation;
- water-efficiency technology and precision irrigation assessment;
- water-infrastructure finance and water-right documentation review.

Do not provide a legal conclusion about water rights. Request infrastructure, consumption, tariff, and rights information needed for a credible review.

## H. Procurement and input costs

Potential triggers:

- material or rapidly rising fertiliser, chemicals, seed, feed, packaging, fuel, diesel, equipment, PPE, or consumable costs;
- adverse price/usage variances, fragmented suppliers, duplicated categories, or weak purchasing controls.

Potential services:

- spend cube and category baseline;
- price-volume-mix and usage analysis;
- bulk purchasing, supplier renegotiation, strategic sourcing, and alternative-supplier review;
- supplier-finance and contract optimisation.

Avoid labelling spend inefficient without volume, specification, quality, timing, or price evidence.

## I. Labour and human capital

Potential triggers:

- material or rising wages, salaries, seasonal labour, overtime, contractor expense, or payroll errors;
- labour cost moving faster than output, yield, packout, hectares, or revenue;
- weak workforce information or manual payroll processes.

Potential services:

- workforce and seasonal-labour planning;
- payroll and overtime review;
- productivity and crew/block analysis;
- HR systems, payroll automation, benefits, and incentive-structure design.

Account for labour law, seasonality, worker welfare, quality, and safety. Cost reduction alone is not evidence of a viable workforce action.

## J. Operations and farm profitability

Potential triggers:

- gross-margin erosion, losses, volatile yields, weak packout, wastage, spoilage, adverse crop/product mix, input overconsumption, labour inefficiency, or low equipment utilisation;
- inability to identify contribution by farm, block, crop, variety, grade, product, or channel.

Potential engagement: **Farm Profitability & Cost Optimisation Programme**.

Typical work:

- product, farm, block, and crop profitability;
- yield, packout, quality, price-volume-mix, and crop-mix variance;
- labour efficiency and input-consumption benchmarking;
- overhead allocation, break-even analysis, and scenario modelling;
- equipment, wastage, spoilage, and production-efficiency review.

This programme should absorb overlapping generic cost-reduction, margin-improvement, and profitability opportunities unless a separate workstream has distinct evidence and value.

## K. FP&A and management reporting

Potential triggers:

- absent or weak budgets, forecasts, cash-flow visibility, cost-centre reporting, farm/block reporting, or board information;
- unexplained budget variances, recurring liquidity surprises, or management accounts that cannot isolate production drivers.

Potential services:

- outsourced FP&A and management accounts;
- integrated budgeting, forecasting, and rolling 12-month cash flow;
- dashboards, KPI reporting, and board packs;
- farm, block, crop, product, and channel profitability reporting.

Do not infer that reporting is weak merely because a budget or dashboard was not included in the uploaded pack; frame absence as an information gap unless management confirms it.

## L. Technology and agritech

Potential triggers:

- manual or fragmented accounting, payroll, inventory, production, irrigation, yield, machinery, or reporting processes;
- material reconciliation problems or lack of operational data needed to manage value drivers.

Potential services:

- farm-management software or ERP selection and implementation;
- accounting, payroll, inventory, and production-system integration;
- production and yield tracking, dashboards, and AI analytics;
- IoT, soil sensors, irrigation automation, drone monitoring, precision agriculture, and machinery telemetry feasibility.

Recommend technology only where a defined business problem, implementation owner, data requirement, and value case exist.

## M. Asset management and valuations

Potential triggers:

- material land, buildings, orchards, vineyards, biological assets, livestock, plant, machinery, packhouses, irrigation, or equipment;
- stale, missing, inconsistent, heavily depreciated, idle, encumbered, or acquisition/disposal-related asset records;
- financing, insurance, succession, transaction, or reporting requirements.

Potential services:

- property and agricultural-land valuation;
- biological-asset and livestock valuation;
- equipment, insurance, and financing valuation;
- business, estate, and succession valuation;
- asset-register verification and utilisation review.

Never infer market value from book value without a supportable valuation basis.

## N. ESG and sustainability

Potential triggers:

- export or retailer/offtaker requirements;
- energy-, water-, fuel-, chemical-, or labour-intensive operations;
- disclosed climate, supply-chain, or sustainability obligations.

Potential services:

- ESG strategy and sustainability reporting;
- carbon-footprint and carbon-credit feasibility;
- climate-risk assessment, water stewardship, and energy transition;
- supply-chain sustainability and export-compliance readiness.

Do not claim carbon-credit eligibility, export obligations, or compliance status without evidence and current criteria.

## O. B-BBEE, ESD, and transformation

Use where South African context and ownership, procurement, funding, customer, or strategic objectives support it.

Potential services:

- B-BBEE strategy and ownership planning;
- enterprise and supplier development and ESD structuring;
- transformation finance, agricultural empowerment, and strategic-partnership assessment.

Verify current codes, thresholds, ownership facts, and funding criteria before expressing outcomes.

## P. Corporate finance

Potential triggers:

- expansion, acquisition, sale, succession, shareholder exit, management buyout, restructuring, merger, or minority-investment intent;
- funding needs, concentrated ownership, ageing principals, dormant assets, or group complexity supported by evidence.

Potential services:

- transaction readiness and valuation;
- integrated financial model and investment case;
- capital raising, acquisition, sale, or minority-investment process;
- shareholder exit, management buyout, restructuring, and family-succession planning.

Do not infer transaction intent from financial statements alone. Present an information request or strategic option when intent is unknown.

## Q. Business structuring

Potential triggers:

- agricultural groups operating through trusts, property companies, farming companies, operating entities, packhouses, equipment companies, or family holdings;
- material related-party balances, undocumented flows, cross-guarantees, concentrated asset risk, tax leakage, succession needs, or governance complexity.

Potential services:

- group and HoldCo/OpCo structure review;
- asset-protection and succession-structure design;
- tax-structure and intercompany-agreement review;
- governance, cash-flow, service, lease, and funding-flow mapping.

Identify potential inefficiency or risk, but do not give legal conclusions. A restructure requires tax, legal, accounting, financing, and implementation analysis before recommendation.

## Consolidation guide

Merge findings when they point to one engagement, for example:

- margin erosion + input inflation + labour inefficiency + overhead growth -> Farm Profitability & Cost Optimisation Programme;
- overdraft dependence + seasonal deficit + fragmented asset finance -> Seasonal Funding & Debt Restructuring Programme;
- export receipts + FX losses + imported inputs -> Treasury & FX Risk Programme;
- electricity + irrigation pumping + generator diesel + capex need -> Energy & Irrigation Efficiency and Funding Review.

Keep opportunities separate when they require different decision makers, evidence, implementation paths, and value mechanisms.
