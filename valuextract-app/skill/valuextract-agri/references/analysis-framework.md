# Agricultural financial analysis framework

Use this reference during evidence extraction and financial analysis. It is designed for farming businesses, agricultural groups, crop and livestock producers, packhouses, processors, exporters, cooperatives, holding companies, farming SPVs, and family-owned groups.

## 1. Source inventory

Catalogue all supplied sources before analysis:

- annual financial statements and their notes;
- management accounts and detailed income statements;
- trial balances, general ledgers, and expense analyses;
- budgets, forecasts, and cash-flow forecasts;
- fixed-asset registers and loan or lease schedules;
- production, crop, yield, packout, and livestock statistics;
- debtor and creditor ageings and inventory reports;
- farming-operation information, company profiles, and website content.

For each source, capture document name, entity, period, basis, currency, units, version, and page/section availability. Resolve obvious duplicates and identify whether the information is consolidated, entity-level, farm-level, block-level, crop-level, or product-level.

## 2. Extraction checklist

### Income statement and detailed expenses

Extract revenue, other income, cost of sales, gross profit, EBITDA, EBIT, tax, and net profit. Inspect individual expense lines, especially:

- employee costs, wages, salaries, seasonal labour, overtime, and contractors;
- repairs and maintenance, equipment repairs, and outsourced services;
- electricity, water, fuel, diesel, and generator costs;
- fertiliser, chemicals, seed, feed, PPE, and consumables;
- packaging, freight, transport, cold storage, logistics, and commissions;
- crop losses, wastage, spoilage, insurance, rent, and professional fees;
- interest, finance costs, bank charges, forex gains/losses, and depreciation.

Look for large balances, rapid changes, duplicate or fragmented categories, abnormal ratios, procurement concentration, unexplained variances, and costs that may be affected by production volume or seasonality. Do not call a cost inefficient solely because it is large.

### Balance sheet

Extract and classify:

- land, buildings, orchards, vineyards, bearer plants, biological assets, livestock, investment property;
- tractors, vehicles, machinery, equipment, irrigation, packhouses, cold rooms, solar assets;
- trade debtors, export receivables, inventory, produce inventory, cash, investments, and intercompany loans;
- mortgages, bank loans, seasonal or production facilities, revolvers, overdrafts, asset finance, instalment sales, leases, shareholder and related-party loans;
- trade creditors, tax, VAT, deferred tax, other liabilities, share capital, retained earnings, and reserves.

Note current/non-current classification, security, impairment, fair-value movements, ownership, related-party balances, maturity, and whether assets are operating, idle, encumbered, or held in separate entities when disclosed.

### Cash flow

Extract cash generated from operations, working-capital movements, capital expenditure, new borrowing, debt repayment, dividends, tax payments, investing flows, financing flows, and closing cash. Calculate operating cash conversion and free cash flow only after defining the measures. Identify seasonal liquidity needs and reliance on overdrafts or production facilities.

### Notes to the financial statements

Inspect accounting policies and notes for property, land, buildings, biological assets, bearer plants, livestock, machinery, equipment, finance agreements, interest rates, security, bonds, guarantees, cessions, related parties, contingencies, commitments, tax balances, deferred tax, leases, directors, ownership, and subsequent events. Notes often contain the decisive evidence for financing, structuring, tax, valuation, and risk opportunities.

## 3. Agricultural-business profile

Determine where evidence allows:

- subsector and primary crop, livestock, processing, or export activities;
- farming region and climate or water context explicitly disclosed;
- production and harvest cycle and the period in which revenue and costs crystallise;
- export versus domestic mix, currency exposure, offtake relationships, and customer concentration;
- owned versus leased land and irrigated versus dry-land operations;
- packhouse, cold-chain, processing, storage, and logistics footprint;
- asset, energy, water, working-capital, and labour intensity;
- group entities, trusts, property companies, operating companies, equipment entities, and intercompany relationships.

Common activities include blueberries, citrus, avocados, macadamias, table or wine grapes, apples, pears, stone fruit, maize, wheat, soybeans, sunflower, sugar, vegetables, livestock, dairy, poultry, and game farming. Never infer the activity merely from a company name.

## 4. Core financial analytics

Calculate only when reliable inputs exist. State the formula and define non-standard measures.

### Profitability

- Revenue growth = `(current revenue / prior revenue) - 1`.
- Gross margin = `gross profit / revenue`.
- EBITDA margin = `EBITDA / revenue`.
- EBIT margin = `EBIT / revenue`.
- Net margin = `net profit / revenue`.
- Return on assets = `profit measure / average total assets` when average balances exist.
- Return on equity = `net profit / average equity` when meaningful.
- Expense growth and each material expense as a percentage of revenue or production.

Reconstruct EBITDA when necessary and disclose the reconciliation and treatment of biological fair-value movements, grants, once-off gains/losses, owner-related items, and exceptional costs.

### Liquidity and working capital

- Current ratio = `current assets / current liabilities`.
- Quick ratio = `(cash + near-cash investments + trade receivables) / current liabilities`, adjusted for context.
- Working capital = `current assets - current liabilities`.
- Debtor days = `average trade debtors / relevant credit revenue * days in period`.
- Creditor days = `average trade creditors / relevant credit purchases or cost base * days in period`.
- Inventory days = `average inventory / relevant cost of sales * days in period`.
- Cash-conversion cycle = `debtor days + inventory days - creditor days`.
- Operating cash-flow coverage and cash conversion should use clearly defined denominators.

Do not use closing balances as averages without saying so. Interpret inventory and receivable days against crop cycles, harvest timing, export settlement patterns, and biological-asset classification.

### Debt and funding

- Debt/EBITDA and net debt/EBITDA, with debt and cash definitions disclosed.
- Debt/equity.
- Interest coverage = `EBIT or EBITDA / finance cost`, specifying which numerator is used.
- Debt-service ability using scheduled principal and interest when available.
- Finance cost as a percentage of revenue.
- Facility maturity concentration, short-term funding dependence, security, interest-rate exposure, covenants, and asset-finance fragmentation.

If EBITDA is negative or close to zero, avoid a misleading leverage multiple and explain why it is not meaningful.

### Asset productivity

- Revenue/total assets, revenue/PPE, and total asset turnover.
- Return on agricultural assets only when the relevant asset and profit bases can be isolated.
- Capex intensity, maintenance versus expansion capex, asset age, idle capacity, and encumbrance when supported.

### Agricultural operating analytics

Where operational data is available, calculate revenue, EBITDA, cost, labour cost, and input cost per hectare; yield per hectare; revenue, cost, and EBITDA per tonne or unit; packout percentage; export percentage; production growth; yield variance; crop or livestock margin; feed-cost ratio; and irrigation/electricity cost ratios.

Keep units, crop varieties, grades, blocks, farms, harvest periods, and biological cycles comparable. A per-hectare or per-tonne metric is invalid when planted, bearing, harvested, or saleable quantities are confused.

## 5. Multi-period and management-account analysis

When multiple periods exist, analyse revenue, gross margin, EBITDA, operating expenses, working capital, debt, cash, capex, finance costs, tax, and productivity trends.

For management accounts, compare current YTD with prior YTD, budget, forecast, and the prior financial year as applicable. Reconcile the management-account period to the annual statements where practical. Explicitly flag seasonality, incomplete harvests, delayed export sales, crop failure, weather events, fair-value accounting, and cut-off differences that could distort conclusions.

## 6. Interpretation discipline

- Correlation is not causation: a cost increase does not prove procurement failure or inefficiency.
- Distinguish nominal cost growth from cost per hectare, tonne, animal, or unit when volume data exists.
- Separate structural margin erosion from a single poor season.
- Treat fair-value changes, revaluations, grants, and insurance proceeds separately from recurring operating performance.
- Do not annualise a seasonal YTD result without a supported phasing model.
- Use operational questions to test hypotheses; do not present missing operational facts as findings.
